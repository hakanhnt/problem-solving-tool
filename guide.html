User guide

ProblemLab takes a business problem through eight steps, from a measured gap to a lasting solution. This guide explains the screens, the steps and how to work with the AI coach.

Version 4, November 2026

The first ten minutes

  1. Explore the example case. Open Example Case from the Cases list in the sidebar and move through the steps. You will see what a completed case looks like at each step. The second example case (GDA stock compliance) uses every advanced tool.
  2. Open your own case. Press New, answer the three-question triage if you like, then pick a template or Quick solve.
  3. Write the problem in one sentence. In Step 1, write what happened, where and by how much, and enter the target and actual values. Leave out solutions and causes.
  4. Get suggestions from the coach and verify them. The Coach in the toolbar prepares candidate inputs for your problem. Take the ones that fit with Add to form; every suggestion stays a hypothesis until you verify it.
  5. Watch the status chip. The chip in the step header shows how many criteria are missing; click it to see the list. Move on once the criteria are met.

Principles of the method

The tool works in any field: logistics, marketing, technology, operations, HR, finance. The aim is not to fill in forms but to learn sound thinking techniques by applying them to your own problem.

A problem, a finding and a root cause are different things.
A problem is a measured gap; a finding is a sub-gap proven with data; a root cause is the systemic reason underneath them.
Define first, analyze next, solve last.
Solutions are not discussed in Steps 1–4; alternatives are generated only after the root cause is found.
Look for the root cause in your own house first.
The analysis focuses on gaps in your own processes, measurement and capabilities instead of blaming outside parties.
AI suggests, you verify.
Every coach suggestion is a hypothesis and must be verified with data and with the people who do the work.
The cycle does not end with the decision.
Actions get owners and the KPI is tracked. If it does not close on the target, go back to the root cause analysis, and tie what you learn into the standard (PDCA).

Screen layout

  1. Sidebar. The case list (open, rename, delete, undo), the Dashboard and New buttons, the progress and maturity indicator, and the list of the eight steps. At the bottom are the language switch (TR/EN), theme, Settings, User guide and Intro links. The « button in the header collapses the sidebar to a thin strip of step numbers, and the app remembers your choice. On narrow screens the sidebar opens from the menu button.
  2. Step header. The step number, title and status chip. The chip shows how many criteria are missing, or ✓ Criteria met when all are done. Click it to open the criteria list.
  3. Toolbar. Coach, Mind Check and Questions to ask. Only one panel is open at a time; the coach opens by itself while it prepares suggestions.
  4. Step form. The fields of the step. Core fields come first; advanced analysis tools sit in expandable cards below. Long text fields can be enlarged by dragging their bottom edge.
  5. AI Assistant. A free-form chat at the bottom of the page, in the role of that step's expert.

Intro, language and saving

  • The intro screen appears once in each new browser tab. Turn it off with Don’t show this again, and reopen it any time from the Intro link in the sidebar.
  • Language switches between Turkish and English in one click. Your inputs stay as they are; the example cases are translated as long as you have not edited them. In English mode the AI also answers in English.
  • Saving is automatic and happens only in this browser. Close the page, open it again and continue where you left off. For backups use Settings › Data and backup.

Case dashboard

The Dashboard button in the sidebar shows all your cases on one screen. Your own cases are listed before the examples. Each card shows the maturity status, a progress line for the eight steps, the KPI gap and summaries of actions, root causes, decision and confidence.

The card's button names the next job and takes you straight to that step: Start by defining the problem on an empty case, Continue on a step with missing criteria, Update overdue actions when actions are late, and Open the report when everything is done.

If the summary line shows a number of overdue actions, click it: the actions are listed grouped by case, with their owner and how many days late they are.

The eight steps

The status chip in each step header checks the Done when criteria below. The criteria measure the minimum the method requires, not how many fields are filled; the maturity indicator in the sidebar looks at evidence, verification and KPI confirmation.

Before you start: triage and quick solve

Not every problem deserves the same depth. The triage in the New case window asks three questions: the time and resources a root-cause solution needs, the benefit it brings and the time pressure. Based on your answers it suggests the full flow, a quick solve, delegating, or a deliberate wait.

Quick solve is a single screen for 15–20 minute problems: a short definition, the factor driving the problem, three "whys", a countermeasure, an owner and a due date. If the problem turns out deeper than expected, Promote to the full flow moves the case into the eight steps without losing anything: the definition goes to Step 1, the factor to Step 2, the whys to Step 5 and the countermeasure to Step 7.

1Problem definition

A measurable answer to "what happened?": one statement with no solution, cause or blame in it.

Done when
  • Problem statement written
  • Target and actual entered
  • KPI direction selected

Enter the dimensions (location or unit, period, breakdown) and the KPI gap. The quality chips under the statement give instant feedback; Refine my statement in the coach panel has the coach rewrite your statement to fit the method. You cannot move to later steps until a problem statement is written.

Advanced tools: IS / IS-NOT, change analysis, references

The IS / IS-NOT specification (Kepner-Tregoe) draws the boundaries of the problem: where, when and in which breakdown it occurs, and where it could occur but does not. The true root cause must explain both sides; in Step 5 candidates are tested against this.

Change analysis asks the classic question for deviation problems: what changed when the deviation began?

In the References card you can add reports, data, notes, links or files (.pdf, .docx, .xlsx, .csv, .txt). The coach draws on them in every step and cites them as R1, R2. With ✎ Open you can read a reference, edit its title and text, and delete irrelevant parts; summaries of long texts refresh after editing.

2Business driver mapping

List the main factors driving the result so that they are mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive (MECE).

Done when
  • At least 2 business drivers defined
  • AI suggestions verified

The coach suggests candidate business drivers for your problem; take the ones that fit with Add to form and edit them with your own observations. Check your suspicions with the people who do the work and on site, not at a desk.

3Business driver analysis

Break the most influential drivers into subcomponents and use process metrics to find where the problem sits.

Done when
  • At least 1 subcomponent analyzed

Fill in SIPOC rows for the problematic process steps: supplier, input, process, output, customer. Defects in input quality usually become visible here.

4Problem findings

Drop the assumptions: each finding is a specific gap that has been measured and proven with data.

Done when
  • At least 1 measured finding
  • Every finding has an evidence source
  • Contributions to the gap entered (Pareto)

Verify the coach's finding hypotheses with your own data and enter the numbers yourself. If you enter each finding's contribution to the gap, the app draws a Pareto chart and you start the root cause analysis from the biggest contributor. No solutions are proposed in this step.

5Root cause analysis

Go from symptom to systemic cause; the chain should end in a gap in process, system or capability, not in a person.

Done when
  • At least 1 root cause written
  • Root causes linked to findings
  • At least one supported by data / verified

Start from the most critical finding and build a 5 Whys chain. The fishbone categories (people, method, system, input, measurement, environment) organize possible causes. Map each root cause to the relevant organization principles, note the capability to develop and record its verification status: hypothesis, supported by data, tested, verified.

Advanced tools: alternative branches, test plan

Open alternative cause branches when needed; for example, "why are operator errors high?" becomes a separate chain. A branch not supported by data is closed, with its elimination documented.

The root cause should explain the IS / IS-NOT pattern from Step 1, and a test plan and the expected KPI impact should be written down.

6Countermeasures and decision

Generate alternatives that remove the root cause, compare them against criteria and write the decision and its rationale yourself.

Done when
  • At least 2 alternatives generated
  • Criterion weights sum to 100%
  • Decision and rationale written

The step opens with containment (8D discipline D3): the measure that protects the customer and the process today while the analysis continues, with its owner and the condition for removing it. Containment buys time; it does not solve the problem, and the decision can never be the containment itself.

Generate at least three alternatives using different thinking methods (first principles, lateral, systems, design…). Give the criteria weights that add up to 100 and score each alternative 0–5 in the decision matrix. The weighted total is calculated for you; if two alternatives are neck and neck, a sensitivity warning appears. The matrix does not decide: Get a decision suggestion from the coach gives you an assessment, and you write the final decision and how it removes the root cause.

The Action Plan follows the decision: give each action an owner, a due date, and impact and effort scores from 1 to 5. The app assigns a priority badge (quick win, strategic, small win, questionable); Sort by priority reorders the plan. The coach's action suggestions state which finding and root cause they address (such as B2, KN1).

Decision timing, second-order effects and the outside view

The Decision timing card asks when the decision should be made. A decision that is cheap to reverse is ASAP: make it now or try it with a small test. One that is expensive to reverse is ALAP: analyze until the opportunity window closes. The opportunity window and stop signal fields set limits against endless analysis.

The And then what? field on the decision card asks about second-order effects, the new problems the solution may create. The Outside view asks how long similar initiatives actually took in your organization or industry; this field is deliberately left without AI, because only you know your organization's history.

When you choose a thinking method for an alternative, the bias it counters and five questions to put to the team open right below it.

Testing the decision: thinking check, devil's advocate, pre-mortem

The Pre-Decision Thinking Check asks three questions: what am I assuming, what other explanation is possible, and who pays the price and when. The Thinking bias scan on the same card reads your case and shows which biases it bears traces of, quoting your own sentences.

The Devil's Advocate opens once the decision is written. Refute my decision produces 3–4 objections based on gaps specific to your case and states the evidence needed to refute each. You answer each objection one by one; an unanswered objection appears as a warning in the report.

The Pre-Mortem (Gary Klein) asks: "It is six months from now and the decision failed; what happened?" The coach produces failure scenarios, early warning signals and preventive measures; add the measures you like to the action plan in one click.

The Similar cases panel offers success and failure examples synthesized from industry patterns. No sources are given; use them as inspiration, not precedent. The FMEA (severity × occurrence × detection) and force field analysis (Lewin) in the advanced section make the solution's risks visible before you implement it.

7Tracking and retrospective

Close the loop: track the progress of the actions and whether the KPI closes on the target.

Done when
  • Action statuses marked
  • At least 1 KPI measurement entered
  • Retrospective written

Mark actions as pending, in progress, done or overdue, and enter the KPI period by period. The measurements turn into a trend chart that shows the remaining gap to target. If the trend does not close on the target, the root cause or the countermeasure is wrong; go back to Step 5 and update the analysis.

Tripwires let you agree on the response now rather than in a crisis: a measurable condition, a check date and a pre-agreed response. The app warns you when a check date has passed. The retrospective has four questions: was the root cause right, did the countermeasures work, was the process right or just the outcome, and what will we make standard.

8Case report

All your inputs come together in one shareable report.

Done when
  • There is content to report (problem statement and at least 1 root cause)
  • Traceability issues resolved

The bar at the top has three views. The Full report shows every section with its charts. The A3 summary is a one-page Toyota A3 layout; print it on A3 landscape paper. The Case map draws the links from KPI to tracking and marks broken nodes; click a node to open its step.

Outputs: Print / PDF prints only the report, with all charts. Download Word produces an editable .docx and Download HTML a single-file page. Share link copies a read-only link; the case data is compressed into the link itself and never goes to a server, which is why the link is long. The recipient can continue with Copy to my cases and edit.

Strengthen the report has two tools: the Executive summary writes a short summary for the top of the report; the Consistency audit finds contradictions and broken links between the steps, with citations (B1, KN2, A1…), and stays on screen only. Open Report contents to choose sections and enter your organization or unit name.

The AI coach

Coach
Opens from the toolbar. When you enter an empty step it runs by itself and produces candidate inputs and verification questions based on your problem definition and earlier steps. Each suggestion goes into the form with Add to form; Suggest again brings fresh ones and + More suggestions adds candidates different from the earlier ones.
Verification badges
Drivers, analyses, findings and root causes added from the coach carry an orange ⚠ badge meaning they are AI suggestions that have not been verified. After checking them against data, click the badge to mark them verified. If unverified items remain, you get a warning when moving to the next step.
Mind Check
Sits in the toolbar in Steps 1–7. It shows the biases people fall into most often in that step, their antidotes and questions to ask yourself. The same content is added to the coach's prompts.
Questions to ask
Lists the questions for the step that you should ask yourself and your stakeholders; use it to prepare for meetings.
AI Assistant
The chat at the bottom of the page. Evaluate my inputs in this step and Generate suggestions and the right questions turn the most common requests into one click. The small AI buttons next to fields ask for help with that field.
The golden rule
AI output is a hypothesis. Verify what you add with your own data and leave out what does not fit your problem. To prevent made-up citations, similar cases and the bias scan never produce sources, names or links.

Settings

Settings at the bottom of the sidebar opens a window with four tabs. Changes are saved immediately; Esc or a click outside the window closes it.

Coach

  • Coaching level: Teaching gives no suggestions and asks Socratic questions; Balanced suggests hypotheses with verification questions; Accelerating produces complete drafts.
  • Automatic suggestions on entering a step: turn it off and the coach runs only when you press its button.
  • Domain / industry context: for example "retail logistics". The coach and assistant tailor their examples to it.
  • Response style: length (short bullets, detailed explanations), tone (formal, friendly coach), criticality (gentle feedback, tough auditor).

AI connection

  • In Automatic mode you do not need to enter anything; the site owner's key on the server is used and never reaches your browser.
  • Your own key: MiniMax, OpenAI or Anthropic. The key is stored only in this browser and sent directly to the provider you choose.
  • Custom / Local: any OpenAI-compatible endpoint: OpenRouter, a corporate gateway, Azure OpenAI, or Ollama and LM Studio on your computer. Preset profiles fill in the address and headers. Subscriptions such as Claude Pro/Max or ChatGPT Plus are not available to third-party apps; you need a credit- or key-based service.
  • Advanced generation settings are collapsed: model name, reasoning effort, analysis depth (standard, broad, deep) and token budget, creativity (consistent 0.2, balanced 0.6, creative 0.9), temperature and top_p. A range of 0.3–0.7 is recommended for structured output; deep analysis takes noticeably longer.

Organization principles

Edit or delete the 20 default principles, or write your own organization's values. The list is used in root cause mapping, coach suggestions and the report. When you delete a principle, the mappings in root causes update automatically.

Data and backup

This tab shows when the cases were last saved and last backed up; the app reminds you when the backup is older than seven days. Download backup downloads all cases and principles as a single JSON file. Choose file adds a backup or a colleague's file; your existing cases are not deleted.

Thinking methods and the biases they counter

Every sound thinking method is a counterweight to a particular thinking error. These errors are not a lack of intelligence or knowledge but a fact to be managed: the mind prefers what is fast and familiar over what is right. The app builds this pairing into the method choice in Step 6, the Mind Check and the bias scan.

Thinking methodBias it countersSign of the bias
Critical thinkingConfirmation biasData supporting the idea is collected; contradicting data is dismissed.
First-principles thinkingStatus quo bias, sunk costThe familiar is chosen over the better; "we have invested so much" becomes the defense.
Design thinkingRepresentativeness biasResemblance to a mental pattern replaces field observation.
Lateral thinkingOverconfidence biasOne solution is taken as right and no alternatives are generated.
Second-order thinkingShort-termism, outcome biasA decision is judged by its outcome rather than its process.
Systems thinkingAvailability bias, oversimplificationA memorable event is taken as common; a layered structure is reduced to one cause.
Algorithmic thinkingAnchoring, heuristic shortcutsThe first number or idea heard shapes every later judgment.

Three behaviors for leaders

  • Being able to say "I don't know". It is not weakness but a trigger that opens up thinking; when the leader does it, the team starts thinking too.
  • Stating your own assumption. "My current assumption is this, and it may be wrong" starts the discussion.
  • Making changing your mind normal. Without it, people defend their positions rather than their ideas.

Decision hygiene: six principles of action

Triage
If the benefit does not justify the cost, skip the full analysis: delegate, solve it quickly or wait on purpose. New case window
ASAP / ALAP
Make decisions that are cheap to reverse now; delay expensive ones with analysis until the opportunity window closes. Step 6, decision timing
Stop signal
Define in advance the sign that you have enough information; when it appears, move to action. Step 6
Second order
"And then what?" Think about the new problem the solution creates at the moment of decision. Step 6, decision card
Outside view
Correct your timing and impact expectations by looking at how similar initiatives actually turned out. Step 6, decision card
Tripwires
Agree on "if X happens, we do Z" in advance, with a calm head. Step 7

The simplest rule: slow down the moment you notice you are thinking fast. The aim is not perfect thinking but catching the moment you think wrong, early.

Frequently asked questions

The coach does not produce suggestions.

Make sure the problem statement in Step 1 is filled in and press Try again in the Coach panel of the toolbar. If you see a key error, the panel's Open connection settings button takes you straight to the right tab.

Will I lose my data?

Your data is tied to this browser and is never sent to a server. If you clear browser data, use a private window or switch devices, you lose access to it. Take regular backups of important cases from the Data and backup tab.

How do I share with my team?

There are three ways: export a PDF or Word file from Step 8; send a read-only link with Share link; or, to hand a case over, share the backup file and have the other person add it with Choose file. For confidential content, prefer a PDF to a link.

Charts are missing from the PDF.

Report charts print regardless of the browser's "background graphics" setting. If they are missing, an old version may be cached; reload the page with Ctrl+F5 (Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac).

The quick solve was not enough.

Promote to the full flow on the quick solve screen moves the case into the eight steps; everything you entered carries over to the matching steps.

The countermeasure did not work. What now?

Do not start over. If the trend in Step 7 does not close on the target, go back to Step 5 and review the root cause, generate new alternatives in Step 6 if needed, and record this in the retrospective.

I broke the example case.

Example cases cannot be deleted. With the example open, Reset example at the bottom of the sidebar returns it to its original state.

The sidebar disappeared.

It may be collapsed. Press the » button at the top of the thin strip on the left of the screen; on narrow screens use the menu button in the top bar.
